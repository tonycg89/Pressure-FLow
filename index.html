Based on 400W panels, about 1,500 kWh/kW/year CA solar output, and residual soiling loss that falls with cleaning frequency (annual about 1.5%, biannual about 1.3%, quarterly about 1.15%) relative to the uncleaned baseline for this environment (10%).

Recovered production is the physical kWh gained from cleaner panels. Usage-adjusted energy value converts that dollar value into retail-equivalent kWh after splitting recovered production between self-consumption valued at the estimated $0.345/kWh CA retail rate and grid export, valued at retail under grandfathered NEM 2.0 or about $0.065/kWh under NEM 3.0 / Net Billing Tariff. Cleaning cost assumes $8/panel per visit with a $125 minimum.

Sources and assumptions
  1. NREL: soiling losses typically 3-5%, ranging up to 25% in high-soiling U.S. locations including parts of California.
  2. Kleissl et al., UC San Diego: 186 California solar sites showed 7.4% efficiency loss after 145 days without rain or cleaning.
  3. Aurora Solar / NREL soiling model: three cleanings per year reduces average annual soiling loss to roughly 1.2%.
  4. U.S. EIA and California Public Utilities Commission: California average residential electricity rate about $0.345/kWh in 2026.
  5. Industry pricing guides: professional solar panel cleaning typically runs $5-$15 per panel, with a $100-$175 minimum service fee.
  6. California Public Utilities Commission / Aurora Solar: Net Billing Tariff exported solar is compensated at an avoided-cost rate averaging about $0.05-$0.08/kWh.
  7. EnergySage: typical California residential electricity usage averages roughly 940 kWh per month.
